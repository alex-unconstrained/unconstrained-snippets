# Higher Education Reference Framework, 2023–2026: What Leading Universities Are Doing

Scope: higher education worldwide, 2023 to September 2026. Every example below comes from a source I found during this research, and each one has a URL and date. Evidence ratings: **Strong** means randomized or large controlled evidence, or large representative surveys. **Emerging** means small RCTs, preprints, quasi-experiments or single-site studies. **Anecdotal** means announcements, press reports or institutional self-reports.

Caveat on method: my web search budget ran out near the end of the research. A few items therefore rest on search-result summaries plus one fetched page rather than two independent sources. They are marked "[single-source]". I did not include anything I could not trace to a named source.

## 1. Themes

### Theme 1: Institution-wide AI provision (whole-system AI licences are now normal)

**The shift.** Between 2023 and 2025, universities moved from banning AI to tolerating it. Since 2025, leading institutions have been buying enterprise AI for every student and staff member. The main reasons they give are equal access (so paying students don't get an advantage), data protection, and signalling that the institution is "AI-ready". The field is now asking a different question: provision has not produced agreement that AI helps education, and contracts, costs and faculty consent are being argued over openly.

**Exemplars**
- **California State University (23 campuses).** Rolled out ChatGPT Edu to more than 460,000 students and 63,000+ faculty and staff. It was announced as the "largest implementation of ChatGPT by any single organization". It is part of an "AI-empowered university" partnership with OpenAI, Google, Microsoft, AWS, NVIDIA, Adobe, LinkedIn and others. Announced February 2025 ([Campus Technology, 5 Feb 2025](https://campustechnology.com/articles/2025/02/05/california-state-university-launches-systemwide-chatgpt-edu-deployment.aspx); [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/openai-and-the-csu-system/)).
  - CSU's own survey of fall 2025 drew **94,060 respondents**. 95% had used at least one AI tool, but **65% of students and 59% of faculty doubt AI benefits education overall**, and 18% of faculty forbid student AI use ([Inside Higher Ed, 2 Apr 2026](https://www.insidehighered.com/news/tech-innovation/artificial-intelligence/2026/04/02/despite-skepticism-widespread-ai-use-cal)).
  - The original contract was about $17M. EdSource reported a renewal of about **$13M a year for three years** amid faculty criticism ([EdSource, 2026](https://edsource.org/2026/cal-state-renews-controversial-system-wide-contract-with-openai/758919); [NPR, 25 May 2026](https://www.npr.org/2026/05/25/nx-s1-5772820/artificial-intelligence-education-technology-california-state-university)). [Contract figures single-source: from search summaries, because the EdSource page blocked direct access.]
- **University of Oxford.** First UK university to give ChatGPT Edu (GPT-5) to all staff and students. This followed a year-long pilot with about 750 researchers and staff, within a five-year OpenAI partnership ([Oxford, 19 Sep 2025](https://www.ox.ac.uk/news/2025-09-19-oxford-becomes-first-uk-university-offer-chatgpt-edu-all-staff-and-students)).
- **Anthropic "Claude for Education".** Launched with LSE, Northeastern and Champlain College. It includes a **"learning mode"** that guides students' reasoning instead of giving answers ([Anthropic, 2 Apr 2025](https://www.anthropic.com/news/introducing-claude-for-education)).
  - Other campus-wide deals: **Duke** gives ChatGPT Edu to all members alongside its self-hosted DukeGPT ([Duke, May 2025](https://today.duke.edu/2025/05/duke-expands-suite-secure-duke-managed-ai-services-openai)). **Arizona State** gives ChatGPT Edu to all students and staff ([ASU](https://tech.asu.edu/node/1232406)).
- **Tecnológico de Monterrey (Mexico), the closest Latin American parallel.** Built its own platform, **TECgpt** (launched to the Tec community September 2023). It created the AI Global Education Network (AIGEN) to share TECgpt with other universities, starting with a 10-university pilot ([GlobeNewswire, 29 Jan 2025](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2025/01/29/3017377/0/en/To-Promote-AI-Adoption-in-Education-Tecnol%C3%B3gico-de-Monterrey-Creates-a-Global-Network-and-Shares-Its-TECgpt-Platform.html); [TecScience](https://tecscience.tec.mx/en/tech/tecgpt/)).

**Evidence strength.** Adoption: **strong**, from large surveys. Learning impact of blanket provision: **anecdotal**, since nobody has published outcome data for system-wide licences.

**Tensions and risks**
- Vendor lock-in and recurring costs during budget cuts. The AAUP and the Academe Blog frame CSU's spend as "AI centrism" at the expense of instruction ([AAUP, Spring 2026](https://www.aaup.org/issue/spring-2026/cal-states-war-working-class-education); [Academe Blog, 4 Jun 2026](https://academeblog.org/2026/06/04/paying-the-costs-of-ai-centrism-csu-re-ups-chatgpt/)).
- Faculty scepticism.
- Access without pedagogy.

**K-12 implication.** Many students will arrive at university with an institutional AI account on day one. Schools should decide whether they provide a governed tool themselves or leave access to whoever can pay. Estonia's AI Leap is the national K-12 precedent: ChatGPT Edu and Claude for 20,000 upper-secondary students and 3,000 teachers from September 2025 ([TI-Hüpe](https://tihupe.ee/en/estonia-launches-nationwide-ai-leap-education-program-and-introduces-learning-supportive-ai-in-upper-secondary-schools/)).

### Theme 2: AI literacy or "fluency" as a graduation expectation for every student

**The shift.** Universities are moving from optional workshops to AI competence for every student in every discipline. This is often framed as "fluent in your field and in AI within your field". China is doing this through national policy. Western universities are doing it through general education requirements.

**Exemplars**
- **Ohio State University.** Every undergraduate from the **Class of 2029** is to graduate "AI fluent". GenAI basics are in the required General Education Launch Seminar and the First Year Success Series. Each college must produce a plan during 2025–26 ([OSU News, June 2025](https://news.osu.edu/ohio-state-launches-bold-ai-fluency-initiative-to-redefine-learning-and-innovation/)).
  - Update: an AI Fluency Faculty Advisory Council was formed in November 2025. A faculty teaching endorsement comes with a $300 incentive. OSU plans **100 new AI-expert tenure-track faculty over five years** ([OSU OAA, 10 Nov 2025](https://oaa.osu.edu/news/2025/11/10/ai-fluency-ohio-state-november-2025)).
- **China: Ministry of Education and leading universities.** Zhejiang University made an introductory AI course mandatory for undergraduates from 2024. April 2025 national guidelines raise AI to a "public foundational course" for all students ([MIT Technology Review, 28 Jul 2025](https://www.technologyreview.com/2025/07/28/1120747/chinese-universities-ai-use/); [China Daily, 25 Mar 2025](http://education.chinadaily.com.cn/2025-03/25/c_1081777.htm)).
  - **Tsinghua** issued university-wide Guiding Principles on AI in education. Instructors must define and disclose AI use per course. Students may use AI as a learning aid but must not copy or "mechanically paraphrase" AI output ([Tsinghua via PR Newswire, 1 Dec 2025](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/tsinghua-university-releases-the-first-university-wide-framework-governing-ai-in-teaching-and-research-302628933.html)).
- **Russell Group (24 UK research universities).** Its joint principles commit members to making students and staff "AI-literate" and adapting teaching and assessment ([Russell Group, 3 Jul 2023](https://www.russellgroup.ac.uk/policy/policy-briefings/principles-use-generative-ai-tools-education)).
- **Unicamp (Brazil).** The University Council approved a GenAI resolution: AI is a support tool, not a substitute for human intellectual work. The university offered **40 courses with AI approaches** in the first semester of 2026 and launched an AI portal and observatory ([Unicamp, 19 Jun 2026](https://www.unicamp.br/en/noticias/2026/06/19/portal-e-observatorio-sao-lancados-para-orientar-uso-da-inteligencia-artificial/)).

**Evidence strength.** **Anecdotal** so far. These are requirements and plans, not measured outcomes.

**Tensions.** "Fluency" is poorly defined and can mean tool training rather than judgement. Students also report a gap: HEPI finds **68% of UK undergraduates see AI skills as essential**, but under half feel staff prepare them ([HEPI, 12 Mar 2026](https://www.hepi.ac.uk/reports/student-generative-ai-survey-2026/)).

**K-12 implication.** Universities are building AI fluency on top of whatever students arrive with. Schools that teach discipline-specific AI judgement (verifying, critiquing, knowing when not to use it), rather than generic prompting, give students a head start.

### Theme 3: Assessment redesign, from detection to "assurance of learning"

**The shift.** This is the most consequential change in the sector. Leading systems accept that unsupervised take-home work can no longer certify what a student knows. They are dividing assessment into two kinds. **Secure assessment** is supervised: in person, oral, or observed. **Open assessment** allows AI and is designed for learning. Assurance is increasingly planned across a whole degree rather than task by task. Oral exams and vivas are returning.

**Exemplars**
- **University of Sydney, "two-lane" approach.** Lane 1 is secure, supervised assessment of learning, often "must-pass" hurdles. Lane 2 is open assessment, where AI "cannot be banned". Since Semester 1 2025 the default has been that AI is allowed except in exams and tests, and policy only permits AI restrictions in Lane 1 ([Sydney, 27 Nov 2024](https://www.sydney.edu.au/news-opinion/news/2024/11/27/university-of-sydney-ai-assessment-policy.html); [Teaching@Sydney FAQ](https://educational-innovation.sydney.edu.au/teaching@sydney/frequently-asked-questions-about-the-two-lane-approach-to-assessment-in-the-age-of-ai/)).
- **TEQSA, Australia's national regulator.** *Enacting assessment reform in a time of AI* sets out three pathways: assure learning at whole-program level, unit level, or a hybrid. It was written with UQ, Deakin, Monash, ECU, Sydney and UTS, and states that detection cannot guarantee integrity ([TEQSA, Sep 2025](https://www.teqsa.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-09/enacting-assessment-reform-in-a-time-of-artificial-intelligence.pdf)).
- **Oral exams in the Ivy League and beyond.**
  - Cornell uses 20-minute Socratic oral defences in biomedical engineering.
  - Penn's teaching centre describes "a massive shift toward in-person assessments".
  - NYU's Panos Ipeirotis built AI-powered oral exams.
  - UC San Diego launched a three-year study on scaling oral exams.
  - Source: [Fortune, 25 Mar 2026](https://fortune.com/2026/03/25/oral-exams-colleges-anti-ai-teaching-method-gen-z-stare/).
- **Retreat from AI detectors.** **Curtin University** switched off Turnitin's AI-writing detection from 1 Jan 2026 and kept similarity checking ([Curtin](https://www.curtin.edu.au/news/oasis-news/update-on-turnitin-ai-detection-tool/)). Trackers list 50+ universities that have disabled or discouraged detectors. [Count from secondary trackers, not independently verified.]

**Evidence strength.** Policy adoption: **strong**. Oral exams improving learning or validity at scale: **emerging** (UCSD's study is ongoing).

**Scale of the problem.**
- HEPI 2026: **94% of UK undergraduates use GenAI for assessed work**. 12% paste AI text directly into submissions, up from 3% in 2024. 65% say assessment has changed significantly ([HEPI, 12 Mar 2026](https://www.hepi.ac.uk/reports/student-generative-ai-survey-2026/)).
- Gallup/Lumina (US): 57% of students use AI weekly for coursework ([Gallup, 1 Apr 2026](https://news.gallup.com/poll/704090/routine-college-students-despite-campus-limits.aspx)).

**Tensions.** Oral and in-person assessment costs staff time and raises equity issues for anxious, neurodivergent and second-language students. "Open lane" tasks risk becoming assessments nobody trusts.

**K-12 implication.** This is the clearest signal for schools. Students will increasingly be judged by **explaining and defending their thinking live**, and by supervised performance. Schools should build oral defence, presentation, and work shown under observation into regular practice, and teach students to document their process when AI is permitted.

### Theme 4: Evidence on AI tutoring. Design, not access, decides the result

**The shift.** The evidence has matured from opinion to randomized trials. The consistent finding: **purpose-built, pedagogically constrained AI tutors can produce large gains, while unrestricted general chatbots can inflate performance and reduce learning.**

**Exemplars**
- **Harvard, Physical Sciences 2 (RCT, crossover, n=194).** A custom AI tutor built on active-learning principles produced median learning gains **more than double** those of an in-class *active learning* session. The comparison was best practice, not a lecture ([Kestin et al., *Scientific Reports*, June 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-97652-6)). **Strong but single-site and short-duration.**
- **Wharton/Penn team, Turkish high school (RCT, n≈839).**
  - During practice, GPT-4 access raised performance by 48% with the base chatbot and 127% with the tutor version.
  - On the later exam without AI, the base chatbot group scored **0.19 SD worse** than controls. The hint-only "GPT Tutor" group showed no measurable harm (−0.01 SD).
  - Source: [Bastani et al., *PNAS*, June 2025](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2422633122). **Strong.** This is a high-school sample, which makes it directly relevant to schools.
- **Stanford Tutor CoPilot (RCT, 900 tutors, 1,800 K-12 students).** AI coaching *human tutors* raised topic mastery by 4 percentage points, and by 9 points for the least-experienced tutors. It cost about $20 per tutor per year ([Wang et al., 2024 working paper](https://edworkingpapers.com/sites/default/files/ai24_1054_v2.pdf)). **Strong.**
- **Georgia Tech Jill Watson.** A course-grounded virtual TA with answers checked against course material. It answered correctly 78.7% of the time versus 30.7% for a generic OpenAI Assistant. An A/B deployment with 600+ OMSCS students linked it to greater teaching presence and better performance ([Georgia Tech, 2 Sep 2025](https://news.research.gatech.edu/2025/09/02/georgia-techs-jill-watson-outperforms-chatgpt-real-classrooms)). **Emerging.**
- **Newer nuance (preprint).** In an RCT with proctored undergraduates, AI access raised test scores by 0.27 SD, persisting a week later. Students who used AI to *explain* concepts gained. Students who used it to *generate text* lost their gains once AI was removed ([Contractor & Reyes, arXiv, 9 Jul 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08849)). **Emerging.**
- **Cautionary.**
  - MIT Media Lab's EEG study (n=54) reported weaker neural connectivity and poor recall of their own essays among ChatGPT users ("cognitive debt"). It is a preprint, has drawn published critique, and the sample is small ([arXiv, June 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.08872)). **Emerging/contested.**
  - A 2026 meta-analysis of GenAI in programming education found a small, non-significant effect (g=0.14) ([arXiv, May 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.04779)).
  - Broader meta-analyses report medium effects, e.g. g≈0.50 across 36 higher-education studies ([Frontiers in Psychology, 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1758670/full)). The underlying studies vary in quality, so treat these pooled numbers cautiously.

**How students actually use AI.** Anthropic's analysis of 574,740 academic conversations found **about 47% were "direct"**, meaning answer- or content-seeking with minimal engagement ([Anthropic, 8 Apr 2025](https://www.anthropic.com/news/anthropic-education-report-how-university-students-use-claude)).

**K-12 implication.** The Bastani result is the key message for any school board. Giving students an unrestricted chatbot can make practice scores look better while real learning falls. Schools should deploy **tutor-mode tools that give hints rather than answers**, and assess without AI to find out what students have actually learned.

### Theme 5: Admissions. Back to verifiable signals, and structured, AI-resistant applications

**The shift.** As essays become unreliable, selective universities are putting more weight on signals that are harder to fake: standardized tests, structured questions, and demonstrated activity.

**Exemplars**
- **MIT, Dartmouth, Yale, Brown, Harvard** reinstated SAT/ACT requirements in 2024. Dartmouth and Yale said tests *help* identify talented lower-income applicants ([Harvard Crimson, 11 Apr 2024](https://www.thecrimson.com/article/2024/4/11/harvard-sat-act-admissions-requirement/); [Axios, 15 Feb 2024](https://www.axios.com/2024/02/15/yale-sat-test-requirement-reverse)).
- **UCAS (UK).** For 2026 entry, the single personal statement was replaced by three structured questions: why this course, how your studies prepared you, and experience outside education. The total is still 4,000 characters ([UCAS](https://www.ucas.com/applying/applying-to-university/writing-your-personal-statement/how-to-write-your-personal-statement-for-2026-entry-onwards)).
- **Kaplan survey of 220 US admissions offices (Jul–Aug 2025).** Only 2% allow GenAI to write essays, 30% ban it, 68% have no policy. 27% allow it for brainstorming ([Kaplan, Nov 2025](https://kaplan.com/about/press-media/college-admissions-officers-survey-2025-essays-ai)).

**Evidence strength.** Policy changes: **strong**. Claims that tests or structured prompts predict success better: **emerging**, since they rest on institution-internal studies such as Dartmouth's and on Opportunity Insights research.

**Tensions.** Test requirements may disadvantage students without test-prep access. Policies on AI in essays are inconsistent across institutions.

**K-12 implication.** International school students need rigorous externally validated results (IB/AP/SAT), a written voice that is authentically their own, and a record of real activities they can talk about in interview. Schools should teach students their target universities' AI-in-application rules explicitly.

### Theme 6: Micro-credentials, modular funding and lifelong learning entitlements

**The shift.** Governments are rebuilding post-school funding around modules and returning adult learners, not only three- or four-year degrees taken at 18. This is partly driven by AI-related job change.

**Exemplars**
- **UK Lifelong Learning Entitlement.** A loan entitlement equivalent to four years of study, usable up to age 60 and fundable module by module. Applications open September 2026 for courses from **January 2027**. It has been delayed several times ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/apply-for-student-finance-lifelong-learning-entitlement); [House of Commons Library](https://commonslibrary.parliament.uk/research-briefings/cbp-9756/)).
- **Singapore SkillsFuture Level-Up.** Citizens aged 40+ receive a non-expiring S$4,000 credit (from May 2024), up to 90% subsidy for a second full-time diploma, and a training allowance ([MOE Singapore, 4 Mar 2024](https://www.moe.gov.sg/news/press-releases/20240304-new-skillsfuture-level-up-programme-to-boost-mid-career-upskilling-and-reskilling)).
- **Australia Microcredentials Pilot.** A$18.5M. Round 2 funded 48 microcredentials at 25 providers. An independent evaluation is due mid-2026 ([Dept of Education, 29 Aug 2024](https://www.education.gov.au/microcredentials-pilot-higher-education/announcements/successful-universities-announced-deliver-new-microcredentials)).
- **EU Council Recommendation on micro-credentials** (16 Jun 2022, before this report's window but still the framework in force) ([European Commission](https://employment-social-affairs.ec.europa.eu/news/adoption-proposal-recommendation-european-approach-micro-credentials-2022-06-30_en)).
- **Competency-based degrees.** **Western Governors University** has 180,000+ students in self-paced degrees that advance on demonstrated mastery ([Forbes, 21 Jan 2025](https://www.forbes.com/sites/bryanpenprase/2025/01/21/a-deep-dive-into-wgu-and-competency-based-education/)). **Tec de Monterrey's Tec21** competency model dates from 2019 ([Tec](https://tec.mx/en/undergraduate/tec-model)).

**Evidence strength.** Policy: **strong**. Labour-market value of micro-credentials: **emerging**, with evaluations pending.

**Tensions.** Credential inflation, uneven quality, and low take-up by the people who most need retraining.

**K-12 implication.** The "one degree for life" story is weakening. Schools should build students' habits of self-directed learning and the ability to show evidence of their skills (portfolios, projects), not just prepare them for entry exams.

### Theme 7: The labour-market signal. Entry-level work is being reshaped first

**The shift.** Universities' main promise, employability, is under pressure at the graduate entry point.

**Exemplars**
- **Stanford Digital Economy Lab, "Canaries in the Coal Mine".** Employment of 22–25-year-olds in the most AI-exposed occupations is now **about 19% below trend** relative to less-exposed peers, up from 13–15% in 2025. There is no comparable gap for experienced workers. The effect comes through **reduced hiring**, not firing, and appears where AI substitutes for workers, not where it complements them ([Stanford DEL, 12 Aug 2026](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/news/canariesaug26/)).
- **WEF Future of Jobs 2025** (1,000+ employers). Fastest-growing skills: AI and big data, networks and cybersecurity, tech literacy. Alongside them: creative thinking, resilience/flexibility/agility, and curiosity and lifelong learning ([WEF, Jan 2025](https://www.weforum.org/publications/the-future-of-jobs-report-2025/digest/)).
- **Student reaction.** 16% of enrolled US students have already changed major because of AI ([Gallup, 2026](https://news.gallup.com/poll/704087/college-students-weigh-impact-majors-careers.aspx)).

**Evidence strength.** **Strong** as descriptive data; causation is still debated.

**Tension.** It is uncertain whether the effect is AI or macroeconomic hiring conditions. Stanford controls for firm-level shocks.

**K-12 implication.** Routine codified knowledge is what AI replaces. Tacit, experiential and interpersonal capability holds its value. Schools should increase authentic, applied and collaborative work.

### Theme 8: Faculty roles and development

**The shift.** Faculty are being asked to redesign courses, assess differently and model AI use, often with little support. The balance between augmentation and automation is also contested: AI assisting academics versus AI replacing their work.

**Exemplars**
- **EDUCAUSE 2025 AI Landscape Study (788 respondents).** Only **22% of institutions have an institution-wide AI strategy**. Faculty training is the top strategic element (63%). 68% say students use AI more than faculty ([EDUCAUSE, 17 Feb 2025](https://library.educause.edu/resources/2025/2/2025-educause-ai-landscape-study)).
- **AAC&U Institute on AI, Pedagogy and the Curriculum.** A national faculty development programme for campus teams: 124 institutions in 2024–25 and about 175 in 2025–26 ([AAC&U](https://www.aacu.org/newsroom/175-institutions-selected-to-participate-in-aac-u-institute-on-ai-pedagogy-and-the-curriculum)).
- **Anthropic, how educators use Claude (74,000 conversations).** 57% of use is curriculum development. Teaching tasks are 77% augmentation, but **grading is 49% automation**, even though faculty rated grading AI's least effective use. One professor: "Students are not paying tuition for the LLM's time, they're paying for my time" ([Anthropic, 27 Aug 2025](https://www.anthropic.com/news/anthropic-education-report-how-educators-use-claude)).
- **Governance flashpoint: ASU's "Atom".** A $5-a-month Claude-based course builder was assembled from faculty lectures without telling the faculty. Professors called the result "Frankensteinian" ([Inside Higher Ed, 29 Apr 2026](https://www.insidehighered.com/news/tech-innovation/artificial-intelligence/2026/04/29/faculty-concerned-about-asus-new-ai-course)).

**Evidence strength.** **Strong** for surveys. **Anecdotal** for development impact.

**K-12 implication.** Teacher development and teacher consent over AI use are what determine success, not the tool. Schools should agree policies on AI in grading and on reuse of teachers' own materials before deploying anything.

### Theme 9: Wellbeing and the "human" value proposition

**The shift.** When content and tutoring are cheap and always available, campus education has to justify itself through **mentorship, belonging, supervised practice and human judgement**. Meanwhile, students are turning to AI for emotional support.

**Exemplars**
- **Student use of AI for emotional support.** HEPI 2026: about 15% of UK undergraduates use AI for companionship or loneliness. Students report worries about isolation and losing skills ([HEPI, Mar 2026](https://www.hepi.ac.uk/reports/student-generative-ai-survey-2026/)). Jisc: students use AI for mental-health advice and want clearer guidance ([Jisc, 21 May 2025](https://nationalcentreforai.jiscinvolve.org/wp/2025/05/21/student-perceptions-of-ai-2025/)).
- **Dartmouth Therabot.** The first RCT of a purpose-built generative AI therapy bot, with 210 adults. Depression symptoms fell by 51% versus a waitlist. The authors stress clinical supervision is still needed ([NEJM AI / Dartmouth, 27 Mar 2025](https://home.dartmouth.edu/news/2025/03/first-therapy-chatbot-trial-yields-mental-health-benefits)). **Strong for this trial**, but the participants were adults, not a campus deployment.
- **Mental health is the main reason students consider leaving.** Mental health and emotional stress remain the top reasons US students consider stopping out ([Lumina–Gallup State of Higher Education](https://www.luminafoundation.org/resource/the-state-of-higher-education-2025/)).
- **Tsinghua's "Xiaoda".** An AI "growth assistant" given to all incoming first-years for study and life guidance ([Tsinghua](https://www.tsinghua.edu.cn/en/info/1418/14423.htm)). **Anecdotal.**

**Tension.** AI companionship could either help students or deepen isolation. Proof of what "human" campus value adds is thin: it is widely argued but rarely measured.

**K-12 implication.** Schools that visibly invest in relationships, advisory and mentoring, and in-person community are aligning with where higher education is heading. They also need explicit guidance for students on using AI for emotional support.

## 2. Short list for the Board

1. **Harvard's AI tutor beat active learning, more than doubling learning gains** (RCT, 2025). But only because it was purpose-built pedagogically.
2. **Unrestricted GPT-4 made high-school students 0.19 SD worse on the exam without AI**, even though practice scores rose. A tutor version that gave only hints avoided the harm (PNAS 2025). This is the single most important caution for schools.
3. **Cal State gave ChatGPT to 460,000 students. Its own survey of 94,000 people found 65% of students doubt it helps learning.** Access alone is not a strategy.
4. **Sydney's "two-lane" assessment:** secure in-person lane plus AI-open lane, now reflected in Australia's national regulator guidance (TEQSA 2025).
5. **Oral exams are back at Cornell, Penn and NYU** (2026). Explaining your reasoning out loud is the new integrity test.
6. **Ohio State: every graduate from 2029 "AI fluent" in their own discipline.** China made AI a foundational course for all university students.
7. **Young graduates in AI-exposed jobs: employment 19% below trend** (Stanford, Aug 2026). Entry-level hiring is where the change shows first.
8. **Harvard, Yale, Dartmouth and MIT brought back SAT requirements. UCAS replaced the free-form personal statement.** Admissions are moving toward signals that are hard to fake.

## 3. Signals for 2027 and beyond

These are forward-looking inferences drawn from the sources above, not established facts.

- **Degree-level assurance of learning becomes the norm.** TEQSA's program-level pathway points to fewer, higher-stakes secure checkpoints (vivas, capstone defences) and AI-open coursework in between.
- **Scaled and AI-run oral assessment.** NYU is already experimenting with AI voice examiners, and UCSD is studying how to scale oral exams. Expect disputes over the fairness of AI-administered vivas.
- **The debate moves from access to tutor design.** Evidence consistently favours constrained tutors (Harvard PS2 Pal, Bastani's GPT Tutor, Anthropic's learning mode, Jill Watson). Procurement will increasingly specify pedagogical behaviour.
- **Modular, lifelong funding goes live.** The UK Lifelong Learning Entitlement starts January 2027. Singapore and Australia models may be scaled or evaluated (Australia's pilot evaluation is due mid-2026).
- **AI-native and platform institutions.** Examples include rebranded "AI universities" (e.g., Maestro University, Dec 2025, [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maestro_University), anecdotal) and ASU-style products that sell course content directly. Expect more faculty IP and governance conflict.
- **A backlash and cost reckoning over AI licences** (CSU renewal controversy). Institutions will be asked to show learning impact before renewing.
- **Entry-level labour pressure pushes universities toward experiential, apprenticeship-like and tacit-skill learning.**
- **Latin America.** Tec de Monterrey's shared TECgpt network and Unicamp's governance model suggest regional universities, including in Brazil, will formalise AI policy and curriculum through 2026–27. This is directly relevant to Curitiba families choosing Brazilian or Mexican universities.
