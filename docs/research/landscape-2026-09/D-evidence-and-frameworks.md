# Cross-cutting evidence base and competitor frameworks for "Where great schools are right now" (research as of Sept 2026)

**Method and caveats.** I checked sources with web search and page fetches. Several publisher pages (PNAS, Nature, OECD, SSRN, Lancet) blocked direct fetching. Where a number could only be confirmed through a secondary source, I say which one. Items I could not verify in this pass are marked **[UNVERIFIED]**. Evidence strength ratings: **Strong** means multiple RCTs or meta-analyses. **Emerging** means one or a few RCTs, or preprints. **Anticipatory** means survey, correlational, lab or neuro work.

**Headline for the client:** the AI-in-education evidence now points one way. **Design and pedagogy decide whether AI helps or harms learning.** Structured, guard-railed and teacher-mediated uses show large gains. Unguided, answer-giving use gives better practice performance and weaker learning. That makes "how a school designs AI use" the distinctive thing to map, more than "whether it uses AI."

## 2. What improves learning: the evidence

### 2a. AI: positive causal evidence

| Study | Design | Result | Strength |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Kestin, Miller, Klales et al.**, "AI tutoring outperforms in-class active learning", *Scientific Reports* (3 Jun 2025) | Crossover RCT, n=194 Harvard physics undergraduates (Fall 2023). A purpose-built GPT-4 tutor with expert-written scaffolds, compared with already-strong active-learning classes. | Median learning gains were more than double. Effect estimates of **0.73–1.3 SD** (quantile regression, as reported by an ETC Journal review). Students needed less time and reported higher engagement. | Emerging (one elite sample, two topics, short-term). https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-97652-6 ; https://etcjournal.com/2025/11/10/review-of-kestin-et-al-s-june-2025-harvard-study-on-ai-tutoring/ |
| **De Simone et al., World Bank**, "From Chalkboards to Chatbots", Policy Research Working Paper 11125 (May 2025) | RCT in 9 public secondary schools in Edo State, Nigeria. Six weeks, 12 sessions of 90 minutes after school. Students worked in pairs with GPT-4, supervised by teachers, with prompts that pushed reasoning. | About **0.3 SD** overall (about 0.24 in English). End-of-year exams also improved. The programme outperformed about 80% of RCT-evaluated interventions in developing countries. | Emerging–strong (well-designed RCT in a real setting; working paper). https://openknowledge.worldbank.org/entities/publication/15e1ff08-15ae-4f7a-b2a8-d146e6c113ee ; https://voxdev.org/topic/education/how-ai-tutors-improved-learning-nigeria |
| **Wang, Ribeiro, Robinson, Loeb, Demszky (Stanford)**, "Tutor CoPilot" (EdWorkingPaper 24-1054, Oct 2024) | Preregistered RCT: 900 tutors, 1,800 K-12 students from under-served communities. The AI coached *human tutors* in real time. | Students were **4 percentage points** more likely to master topics, and **9 points** more with lower-rated tutors. Cost about $20 per tutor per year. Tutors asked more guiding questions and gave away fewer answers. | Emerging–strong. https://edworkingpapers.com/ai24-1054 |
| **Google LearnLM team and Eedi**, "AI tutoring can safely and effectively support students" (arXiv, 29 Dec 2025) | Exploratory RCT, 165 secondary students in 5 UK schools. The AI drafted messages and human tutors supervised. | Tutors approved 76.4% of AI messages with little or no editing. Students were 5.5 points more likely to solve novel problems (66.2% vs 60.7%). | Emerging (small; the developer co-authored it). https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.23633 |
| **EEF / NFER**, "ChatGPT in lesson preparation" (Dec 2024) | RCT with 259 teachers in 68 English secondary schools (KS3 science). Teachers used ChatGPT plus a guide. | Planning time fell **31%** (56.2 vs 81.5 minutes a week). An independent panel found no difference in quality. | Strong for workload; says nothing about student outcomes. https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/projects-and-evaluation/projects/choices-in-edtech-using-generative-ai-chatgpt-for-ks3-science-lesson-preparation-2024-teacher-choices-trial |
| **Contractor & Reyes**, "Experimental Evidence on the Learning Impact of Generative AI" (arXiv, 9 Jul 2026) | In-person randomised experiment with undergraduates learning material and writing essays. | Knowledge scores rose **0.27 SD**, and the gain persisted a week later. Students who used AI to explain concepts ("augmentation") kept their gains. Students who had AI write for them ("automation") lost their quality gains once AI was removed. | Emerging (preprint). https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08849 |

### 2b. AI: harms and over-reliance

- **Bastani, Bastani, Sungu, Ge, Kabakcı, Mariman**, "Generative AI without guardrails can harm learning: Evidence from high school mathematics", *PNAS* 122(26) (25 Jun 2025). This was a field experiment in a high school, with a standard GPT-4 interface ("GPT Base") and a version that gave teacher-designed hints ("GPT Tutor").
  - Practice performance rose with AI. With the standard version, students did worse on exams once AI was removed.
  - The OECD Outlook 2026 reports this as **+48% task success and −17% on the unaided exam**.
  - The hint-based design largely removed the harm.
  - Sample details (about 1,000 students in Turkey) are **[UNVERIFIED in this pass]**.
  - Strength: strong for the harm mechanism.
  - https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2422633122
- **Fan, Tang, … Gašević**, "Beware of metacognitive laziness", *British Journal of Educational Technology* (2025). Lab RCT comparing ChatGPT, a human expert and checklist support for essay revision. ChatGPT improved the essay score but not knowledge gain or transfer, and learners self-regulated less. Emerging. https://bera-journals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/bjet.13544
- **Kosmyna et al. (MIT Media Lab)**, "Your Brain on ChatGPT: Accumulation of Cognitive Debt" (arXiv, June 2025). EEG study, n=54, three sessions plus a switch session. The LLM group showed the weakest neural connectivity and poorer recall of and ownership over their own essays. Anticipatory: not peer-reviewed, small, and critiqued (arXiv 2601.00856). Useful as a signal, not as proof. https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.08872
- **Gerlich**, "AI Tools in Society: Impacts on Cognitive Offloading and the Future of Critical Thinking", *Societies* 15(6) (Jan 2025; a correction was later issued). Survey plus interviews, n=666. Heavier AI use was associated with lower critical thinking, mediated by offloading, and strongest in younger users. Anticipatory (correlational). https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5082524
- **Lee et al. (Microsoft Research and CMU)**, CHI 2025. Survey of 319 knowledge workers with 936 examples. Higher confidence in GenAI went with less critical thinking; higher self-confidence went with more. Anticipatory. https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/the-impact-of-generative-ai-on-critical-thinking-self-reported-reductions-in-cognitive-effort-and-confidence-effects-from-a-survey-of-knowledge-workers/
- **Caution on meta-analyses.** Wang & Fan (2025, *Humanities and Social Sciences Communications*) reported g = 0.867 for ChatGPT on learning performance. **It has been retracted** because of discrepancies in the analysis. Do not cite it. https://www.nature.com/articles/s41599-026-07310-z

**Synthesis:**
- When AI provides the *effort*, as in answer-giving, automation or unguided chat, practice performance rises and learning falls.
- When AI provides the *scaffold*, as in hints, Socratic questioning, teacher mediation, coaching human tutors, or structured routines, gains look like strong tutoring (about 0.3 SD or more).
- The OECD Outlook 2026 and Brookings 2026 both reach this "pedagogical intent" conclusion independently.

### 2c. Durable evidence that does not depend on AI (the foundation AI should amplify)

- **Tutoring.** Nickow, Oreopoulos & Quan (NBER w27476, 2020; *AERJ* 2024): pooled **0.37 SD** across experimental studies. Effects are larger when teachers or paraprofessionals tutor and when tutoring happens during the school day. https://www.nber.org/papers/w27476
  - Kraft, Schueler & Falken (EdWorkingPaper 24-1031, Oct 2024; *Review of Educational Research* 2026), 282 RCTs. Effects shrink with scale: **about 0.44 SD** for programmes serving fewer than 100 students, **about 0.16 SD** for 1,000 or more. The best practice is in-person, in school, three or more times a week, with groups of three or fewer.
  - Strong. https://edworkingpapers.com/ai24-1031
- **Feedback: +6 months; metacognition and self-regulation: +7 to +8 months.** Both have high evidence security (EEF Toolkit). Strong. Links as in the EEF row above.
- **Retrieval practice.** Adesope, Trevisan & Sundararajan, *Review of Educational Research* 87(3) (2017): **g = 0.61** overall, and similar in classrooms (about 0.67). Strong. https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.3102/0034654316689306
- **Belonging.** Walton, Murphy, Logel, Yeager et al., *Science* 380 (May 2023). RCT with 26,911 students at 22 institutions. A pre-college online exercise of under 30 minutes raised full-time completion of the first year, especially for groups that historically progressed at lower rates, **but only where the context offered real opportunities to belong**. Strong, and it depends on context. https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.ade4420
- **SEL.** Cipriano et al., *Child Development* (2023), 424 studies: consistent positive effects. Strong. See the CASEL row above.

**Usage reality.**
- Pew Research Center (fieldwork Sep–Oct 2025, published 24 Feb 2026, n=1,458 US teens) found:
  - **54%** had used chatbots for schoolwork, and **10%** for all or most of it.
  - **59%** believed classmates cheat with AI at least sometimes.
  - https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2026/02/24/how-teens-use-and-view-ai/
- This was up from 26% using ChatGPT for schoolwork in late 2024 (Pew, 15 Jan 2025). https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/01/15/about-a-quarter-of-us-teens-have-used-chatgpt-for-schoolwork-double-the-share-in-2023/
- Implication: students are already using AI, with or without school design. The school's choice is about the *design* of that use, not about adoption.

## 3. Critiques and risks

1. **Equity.**
   - Gains depend on the conditions in which AI is deployed: devices, supervision, teacher capacity (World Bank Nigeria; Brookings 2026).
   - Tutor CoPilot helped students of weaker tutors most, which suggests AI can narrow gaps *if aimed there*.
   - Pew 2025 found higher ChatGPT schoolwork use among Black and Hispanic teens (31%) than White teens (22%). Who benefits depends on how students use it, not only on access.
   - Brazil's CPP has noted that enforcing the phone law has exposed inequality between schools. https://cpp.org.br/um-semestre-sem-celular-nas-escolas-aplicacao-da-lei-15-100-2025-escancara-desigualdade-educacional/
   - Strength: emerging.
2. **Data privacy and surveillance.**
   - Human Rights Watch, "How Dare They Peep into My Private Life?" (25 May 2022), reviewed 163–164 edtech products endorsed by 49 governments. **89%** surveilled children or could do so. https://www.hrw.org/report/2022/05/25/how-dare-they-peep-my-private-life/childrens-rights-violations-governments
   - In response: the UK DfE product expectations (2025), and Brazil's CNE ban on emotion recognition and social scoring (2026), under the LGPD and the ECA (Brazil's data-protection law and Statute of the Child and Adolescent).
   - Strong (documented).
3. **Teacher deskilling and loss of professional agency.**
   - UNESCO's teacher framework and the OECD Outlook 2026 both warn against AI replacing teacher judgement. The CNE now bars AI from deciding grades or discipline.
   - The counter-evidence: Tutor CoPilot and the LearnLM/Eedi study found AI *upskilled* tutors' questioning.
   - An adjacent example from medicine is often cited: endoscopists detected fewer adenomas after regular AI use (Lancet Gastroenterology & Hepatology, 2025). It is **[UNVERIFIED in this pass]**.
   - Strength: anticipatory for teaching.
4. **Homogenisation.**
   - Doshi & Hauser, *Science Advances* (Jul 2024): AI ideas made individual stories rated more creative, especially for less creative writers, but stories became **more similar to each other**. https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.adn5290
   - Risk for schools: essays, project work and student "voice" converging on the same patterns.
   - Emerging.
5. **Cognitive offloading and assessment validity.**
   - See 2b.
   - With 59% of US teens perceiving AI cheating, unsupervised take-home written tasks lose validity.
   - Brazil now bans AI essay grading and punishing students on the basis of AI detectors alone.
   - Strong for the offloading mechanism; emerging for long-term effects.
6. **Environmental cost.**
   - IEA, "Energy and AI" (Apr 2025): data-centre electricity use was about 415 TWh in 2024 (about 1.5% of global use). It is projected to reach about **945 TWh by 2030**, with AI the main driver.
   - Relevant to sustainability commitments common in international schools.
   - Strong (for the projection's basis). https://www.iea.org/reports/energy-and-ai/executive-summary
7. **Hype cycles and weak evidence.**
   - The retracted Wang & Fan meta-analysis, and headlines such as "2 years of learning in 6 weeks", show that claims run ahead of evidence.
   - Kraft's scale-decay finding (0.44 SD falling to 0.16 SD) suggests pilot effect sizes for AI tutors will also shrink at scale.
   - Many studies are co-authored by vendors (for example LearnLM/Eedi).
   - Strong (a methodological point).

## 4. Recommendation: dimensions for a distinctive "Landscape of Great Schools"

**How to be distinctive rather than a copy of OECD or UNESCO:**
- Describe **observable school practice**, what you would see on an ordinary Tuesday, not learner competency lists.
- Give each dimension **3–4 maturity stages** plus named "anti-patterns".
- Attach an **evidence rating** to each practice, using EEF-style security ratings.
- Cover **K-12, higher education and professional learning** with the same dimensions.
- Build in **cross-walks to external frameworks**, so a school gets AILit, UNESCO, BNCC Computação and CNE/MEC compliance mapped for free.

Candidate dimensions:

1. **Learning design for cognitive ownership ("productive struggle by design").**
   - Covers how AI use is designed so students do the thinking: hint-first rather than answer-giving; augmentation rather than automation; checks of unaided performance.
   - *Rationale:* the single strongest finding of 2024–26 (Bastani; Contractor & Reyes; Kestin; OECD "pedagogical intent"). No existing framework makes it a top-level dimension.
2. **An evidence-based core that AI amplifies.**
   - Covers feedback, retrieval and spacing, metacognition, and tutoring dosage.
   - Asks whether AI is being used to deliver more of these with fidelity (as in Nigeria and Tutor CoPilot) or instead of them.
   - *Rationale:* keeps the framework grounded in strong non-AI evidence and guards against hype.
3. **Assessment integrity and redesign.**
   - Covers valid evidence of learning when AI is everywhere: supervised and oral components, process portfolios, AI-use declarations, and no punishment based on detectors alone.
   - *Rationale:* existing frameworks largely skip assessment. Brazil's CNE now requires this, and it is where schools feel the most pain.
4. **Teacher expertise and professional agency.**
   - Covers AI as a coach and a way to save time (EEF's 31% cut in planning time; Tutor CoPilot), and protected time for collaborative inquiry.
   - Teachers keep authority over judgements; AI never decides grades or discipline.
   - *Rationale:* turns the deskilling risk into an upskilling agenda, and aligns with UNESCO's teacher framework and the CNE.
5. **Belonging, relationships and wellbeing.**
   - Covers human relationships as the non-negotiable core, context-sensitive belonging practices, SEL, phone and attention policy (Law 15.100), and monitoring of AI companionship and dependence.
   - *Rationale:* Walton 2023 shows belonging is causal and depends on context. Brookings flags risks to social and emotional development.
6. **Student agency and AI fluency: critical, creative, civic.**
   - Covers students who can engage, create, manage and shape AI (AILit / PISA 2029), including resisting homogenisation (keeping an original voice) and judging the environmental and social costs.
   - *Rationale:* necessary for PISA alignment. We distinguish ourselves by stressing voice and originality (Doshi & Hauser) and continuity from K-12 through higher education to professional learning.
7. **Governance, safety and data stewardship.**
   - Covers a risk-tiered inventory of tools (mirroring the MEC and CNE four-tier model), vendor standards (DfE-style), LGPD/ECA compliance, age-appropriate access (supervision in early years and Years 1–5), and transparency with families.
   - *Rationale:* legally binding in Brazil from 2026–27. Other frameworks treat this as a separate policy document rather than part of what makes a school great.
8. **Evidence culture and equity of impact.**
   - Covers whether the school tests its own AI initiatives (small internal trials in the EEF style), disaggregates who benefits, and aims AI support at the students and teachers who gain most.
   - *Rationale:* addresses the hype cycle and the decay of effects at scale (Kraft). It turns "leading practice" from a list of tools into a learning organisation. Almost absent from OECD, UNESCO and ISTE.

**For ISC Curitiba specifically:**
- Dimensions 3, 4 and 7 map directly onto the new CNE guidelines (approved 1 Sep 2026, with 12 months to comply).
- Dimension 6 maps onto BNCC Computação (mandatory from 2026) and the IB/international expectations that come with AILit and PISA 2029.
- That gives the framework an immediate compliance-plus-excellence value proposition.
