Inside the experience

Think deeply.
Then make it real.

People bring the work they know. We bring the structure, live modelling and hands-on support to help them move from a recurring problem to a testable solution.

Clarity before building.

Short pre-work surfaces real pain points and introduces data safety. In the room, teams form around ideas they want to take forward.

People leave with more than a demo.

A scoped problem, a prototype or proof of concept, test findings and an impact case to take forward.

An illustrative challenge to shape together

What if recurring programme questions were easier to answer?

A small assistant could prepare a first response from approved programme information, with sources to check and a clear hand-off to a person.

A proposed NESA example, not a previous project or agreed scope.

01

Bring the real question

Where do repeat enquiries consume attention? Agree who needs help and what a good answer requires.

02

Build a small, testable version

Use approved information and synthetic enquiries. Keep a person responsible for checking the draft.

03

Test what matters

Check accuracy, source references and when to say “I don’t know.” Measure reply quality and preparation time in a pilot.

Design the follow-through
before the first day.

CCSD participants asked for more build time and ongoing support. For NESA, we would agree pilot owners, review gates and a 30-day check-in up front, then assess actual use and value.

Evidence, sources & scope

Delivered programme. Cherry Creek School District, Colorado: 19–20 March and 17–18 August 2026. Both cohorts served non-instructional staff. Spring: 23 rostered attendees across eight departments. Summer: 28 enrolments; enrolment is not a verified attendance count.

Survey method. CCSD “Exit Survey (Day 2) — Responses (BOTH events, 39 rows).xlsx”, split by timestamp. Spring n=19; Summer n=20. Q1: overall experience 4.63 and 4.55/5; Q3: facilitation 4.84 and 4.90/5. Pooled means: 4.59 and 4.87/5. Q4–5: confidence 2.47 to 3.89 (Spring), 2.10 to 4.00 (Summer); 17 and 19 respondents respectively reported improvement. All responses are included; one Summer respondent said they assisted several teams. Confidence is retrospective self-report, not a causal estimate of skill gain. The displayed headline “I could do this” / “I built this” is editorial framing, not a participant quotation.

Programme and quotation. Spring and Summer CCSD On-Site Guides; “Hackathons — Source of Truth (UnconstrainED)”, compiled 18 September 2026, sections 1–4, 6 and 11. Anonymous quotation: Spring exit survey Q8, 20 March 2026. The agenda is condensed; breaks and lunch are omitted.

NESA adaptation. The programme-question assistant and 30-day follow-through are proposals to discuss. Prototype adoption, time savings and organisational outcomes would be measured during follow-up. No audited adoption rate or realised financial return is claimed here.
