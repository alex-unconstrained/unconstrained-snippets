01 · What you asked for

Not another binder

You want a partner to lead your strategic direction process, and you don't want a traditional strategic plan: a binder that gets approved, filed, and quietly forgotten.

We agree. Schools move forward when they know where they're heading, why, and how they'll know they're getting there, and when their people recognize themselves in that direction.

We've kept this proposal short on purpose. The detail should come from your community, and we'd rather shape it with you than guess at it.

Why Curitiba gets this

A city named for its pines

Curitiba's name comes from the Tupi-Guarani for "many pines", after the araucária forests that once covered the plateau. The araucária is Paraná's tree, and the azure jay, Paraná's bird, is said to plant the forest: it buries pine nuts to eat later and forgets some, and those become trees.

A school is full of people planting trees whose shade they won't sit in. That is the spirit of the process we're proposing: a direction your people grow themselves, and that keeps growing after we leave.

02 · How we work

The UnconstrainED Tree

We think of a school as a tree its community tends together. Tools, including AI, can help the gardeners. They can't do the growing.

Our own mark shows it: a living trunk with circuitry in its branches. Technology extends a school's reach. It never replaces the trunk. The framework we'll use with ISC is built on that picture.

The trunk: one question at the centre of everything

What does ISC choose to make possible, and for whom?

Every conversation, report and decision in this process comes back to this question. Answering it with your community is the work.

PruneTimeWhat can ISC cut back so new growth has energy?

Removing avoidable work, and deciding where the freed time goes before it fills with more of the same. We prune tasks, never people.

GraftTransformationWhat new practice will take on ISC's roots?

Joining new practice to what already works. We bring cuttings from great schools around the world; only what suits your roots will take.

Grow towardDirectionWhich light is ISC reaching for, and who will sit in the shade?

Choosing the future ISC wants to help create, and naming who it's for: students, families, staff and the Curitiba community.

The roots

Eight principles hold our work up. Among them: Human First (the effect on students, families and staff comes before efficiency), Author Before Tool (people do the thinking; tools extend it), Collaborative Companion (we offer choices, not verdicts) and Expertise with Humility (we say what we recommend, and where we might be wrong).

The soil

A gardener can't make a tree grow; a gardener can make the soil good. Direction takes root only when the conditions around people change too: time, permission, relationships, skills and clear governance. That's why we stay for a year after April.

The methods we'll use, and why

Four proven methods, each chosen for a specific job, plus a deliberate look outward.

01Listening

Empathy interviews

Open, story-based conversations surface what people actually experience on an ordinary school day. That's the raw material for a direction your people believe in.

02Framing and April

Appreciative Inquiry and SOAR

Strengths, Opportunities, Aspirations, Results. Starting from what already works keeps the Board focused on where it wants to go.

03Testing the direction

Three Horizons and futures thinking

Today, the transition, and the future in one picture, stress-tested against plausible futures, including how AI may reshape schooling.

04Facilitation

Liberating Structures

Simple formats such as 1-2-4-All and 15% Solutions give every person in the room a real way in.

And: grafting from other orchards

Beyond our own ecosystem

Alongside these four methods, we bring the world in. From our experience, our network, and our engagement with great schools around the world, we bring ISC's decision makers what leading schools are doing now, so they can see what makes sense and what is relevant to ISC's direction.

It works in both directions. Your community's wants and needs, hopes and fears take on more shape when they're set against what is out there. And outside practice only matters where it meets what your community actually said. A practice that worked elsewhere is a cutting, not a tree: it only takes if it suits your roots.

A question we'll bring to the Board early

If ISC were starting from scratch today, knowing what AI can now do, what would you still choose to do exactly as you do now, and what would you never build again?

The oldest warning about AI in school

Don't pull on the seedlings

About 2,300 years ago, the philosopher Mencius told of a farmer who worried his crop was growing too slowly, so he went out and pulled on every seedling to help it along. By the next morning, every plant had withered.

The research on AI in schools says the same thing. Tools designed to give hints and ask questions can help students learn; tools that simply hand over answers can raise practice scores while students learn less. We'll help ISC design for the first and guard against the second, and protect the pathways by which new teachers and young people grow into expertise.

People stay in charge

The agent drafts and organizes. Our team reads, checks and interprets everything, and the conclusions are ours and yours. AI never decides anything about a student, a grade or a member of staff.

Your data stays protected

Before any listening begins, we agree a data-protection approach with you that complies with Brazil's data-protection law (LGPD): what's collected, where it's stored, who sees it, and how identifying details are removed.

Ready for Brazil's new AI rules

Brazil's National Education Council approved guidelines on AI in education in September 2026. We'll make sure ISC's direction, and our own work, fits them, alongside IB, CIS and NEASC expectations.

No training on your data

We only use AI tools whose terms don't allow them to train on your data.
