Claude at Work, two afternoons

Co-facilitated by Alex Johnson and Gavan Porter

Draft for our call

The same 5.5-hour program, split into two afternoons a week apart, 2–6pm IST on weekdays. Day 1 makes the output trustworthy. Day 2 makes it repeatable. In the week between, each person runs one real process through Claude and logs how it goes, so the skill they build on Day 2 comes from their own work.

Why split it

  • It fits your window.5.5 hours won't fit a 2–6pm afternoon. 2h45 fits, with room to spare.
  • It targets the real problem.People drop Claude when the first pass is wrong and go back to checking by hand. A week of logged runs shows where it's reliable and where a person needs to check.
  • The capstone is real.Each skill comes from a process they've already run several times. Vidya's invoice-to-import-template mapping is the obvious first candidate.
  • Two sessions of practice for you.You run a breakout on both days, which counts as two co-facilitation sessions toward your certification instead of one.

Day 1 · Make it trustworthy

2h45
  1. 0:00–0:15
    OpeningModule 1Gavan

    Each person names the one process they most want off their plate. Gavan explains why the business is doing this: AI working alongside people, not replacing them.

  2. 0:15–1:00
    Prompting + Projects for finance workModule 1

    Set up a Claude Project holding the context they re-type every time (client templates, chart-of-accounts conventions, import formats) plus custom instructions. Prompt patterns that suit bookkeeping.

  3. 1:00–1:40
    Keep going when it's 50% rightModule 1

    The core block. Treat Claude like a new colleague: tell it what it got wrong and iterate. Add a second Claude pass that reviews the output before a person does, then spot-check a sample instead of redoing everything. Worked live on invoice-to-import mapping. Includes five minutes on client data and what stays out of prompts.

  4. 1:40–1:50
    Break
  5. 1:50–2:30
    Artifacts, tracking sheets, Excel vs CopilotModule 2

    Agree one tracking-sheet format for the team and build it as an artifact. Then run the same task in Claude in Excel and in Copilot side by side, including for clients that only allow Copilot.

  6. 2:30–2:45
    Set the weekBridgeGavan breakout

    Each person commits to one process: at least three runs through Claude, the review routine every time, accuracy and time saved logged in the tracking sheet.

Between the days · about a week

What each person does

  1. Run their chosen process through the Day 1 Project at least three times on live work.
  2. Log each run: how accurate it was, what went wrong, how long checking took, time saved.
  3. Bring their best prompt and 2–3 real examples to Day 2, including at least one failure.

Optional: a 15-minute check-in mid-week from Alex or Gavan.

Day 2 · Make it repeatable

2h45
  1. 0:00–0:20
    What the week showedBridge

    Each person walks through their log: where Claude held up, where it broke, what the review caught.

  2. 0:20–1:05
    Cowork + routinesModule 2

    Hand off multi-step work in Cowork on their real files. Set up a routine that runs on a schedule, such as an end-of-day summary for Gavan of what changed across the team's tracking sheets, or a month-end checklist.

  3. 1:05–1:15
    Break
  4. 1:15–2:15
    Capstone: build the skillModule 3Gavan breakout

    Each person turns their repetitive process into a skill, with the review step written in. Gavan runs one breakout room and Alex runs the other. Connectors only where they link to tools the team already uses.

  5. 2:15–2:35
    Test + showcaseModule 3Gavan

    Run each skill on a fresh real example and review it in front of the group. Gavan asks the sign-off question: would you send this to a client?

  6. 2:35–2:45
    CloseBridge

    Who owns each skill, the next process to automate, and a date for a check-in with Gavan (and Alex) about 30 days out.
